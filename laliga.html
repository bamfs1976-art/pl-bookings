How this desk works

Every number here comes from 2025-26 form. The 2026-27 La Liga season starts in mid-August; until it has minutes in it, this is a pre-season book.

La Liga is the most card-heavy of Europe's big five, and it is not close. Over the six seasons to 2025-26 it produced 4.71 yellows a game against the Premier League's 3.64 — 29% more — plus double the reds and the highest yellows-per-foul rate of the five. So the whistle is genuinely stricter rather than the football merely scrappier, and every probability on this desk sits higher than its English sibling for that reason.

The risk score

A player's booking risk is yellows per 90 × 2 + fouls committed per 90. Fouls are the larger term because they are the thing a referee reacts to; yellows are weighted double because they are the outcome being priced. It is the same formula the Premier League desk uses, from the same tested code (assets/core.js), so the two desks cannot drift apart about what risk means.

From risk to a probability

The risk score is mapped through a logistic curve anchored so that the average minutes-weighted player lands on the league's actual base rate — total yellows divided by total player-matches. Fair odds are that probability inverted. They carry no margin, so a bookmaker's price will always be shorter; the gap is his edge, not an error.

The referee ×factor

Choosing an official on the Players tab rescales every probability by his card rate. The factor blends two signals equally: yellows per game against the league average, and cards per foul — which divides out how foul-heavy his fixtures happened to be and is the purer measure of a strict whistle. It is applied on the odds scale and clamped to ×0.75–×1.30, so a strict referee cannot push an already-likely booking to a silly number.

Suspension watch

Spain bans a player for one match at every fifth caution, and then the count restarts with the same penalty — there is no escalating ladder as in England, and no matchday gate, which makes this the busiest of the three desks for a suspension watch: on a full season's form a regular starter is near-certain to be banned at least once. The strip shows how far into his current cycle each player is and the chance he completes it within the next one, three or five matches, from his shrunk yellow rate and the share of a match he tends to play. Before the season starts it shows instead the matchday his fifth caution is projected to land on.

Fixtures, heat and the card markets

Booking heat is the expected number of cards in the match. Every squad player's chance of being carded is weighted by how likely he is to be on the pitch — last season's minutes spread eleven starters across the squad — and the two sides are added together. On the 2025-26 squads it is calibrated against the card rate the division actually produced; the Guide footer below reports the current figure on both sides of that comparison.

The over lines and both teams carded come from the same per-player probabilities, combined exactly rather than simulated. They assume the cards are independent given the referee, which is the usual simplification and is not quite true: a flashpoint produces two at once. Read the over lines as a fair baseline, not a forecast.

Referees come from the feed where the appointment is published — those cards are marked appointed and cannot be overridden here. Where one is not yet named, choose an official and the whole card re-prices; that choice is remembered in this browser only. An appointed referee this desk has no card record for is priced at ×1.00 and says so, rather than quietly guessing.

What “basis” means

  • LL 2025-26 La Liga form. Same division, directly comparable.
  • SEG The clubs promoted this summer, whose 2025-26 form is a Segunda División record. Flagged because a foul rate earned in the second tier is not the same evidence as one earned in the first — and on this league's numbers it will understate them, because La Liga's referees card more readily than Segunda's.
  • NEW No usable record: minutes too few, or no fouls data. Rates are blank rather than zero, because “unknown” and “never fouls” are different claims.

Known limits — read these before betting anything

No live data. The Premier League desk refreshes in-season from the official FPL API, which is free, open and unauthenticated. Spain has no equivalent — the official fantasy game's API is app-only and needs a token that expires daily — so this desk is a static book until it is refreshed. Nothing here knows who is injured, suspended, rested or dropped. The fixture cards name likely candidates from last season's minutes, not from a team sheet — a player who left the club in the summer can still appear on one.
  • The suspension strip rests on a rule I could not read first-hand. RFEF art. 112 sets one threshold — five cautions in a season and competition, one match — and then “cumplida la sanción, se iniciará un nuevo ciclo de la misma clase y con idénticos efectos”. So unlike England there is no ladder: the tenth card costs one match and so does the fifteenth. That is corroborated across several independent quotations of the article, but every attempt to open the Código Disciplinario itself was blocked, so it is not verified from the source document — docs/spain-suspensions.md sets out exactly what was and was not checked, and names the one page to open to close the gap. One secondary source disagreed and is recorded there too.
  • The strip counts this season only. Accumulation does not carry between seasons and each competition counts separately, so Copa cautions never pool with league ones. Until the season has been harvested every player is on zero and the strip says so, forecasting who reaches five first rather than pretending to a live count.
  • No referee career history. The English desks carry 26 seasons of it from a public dataset. There is no Spanish equivalent, so a referee here is described by last season only.
  • The promoted clubs have no club card rate. They were in Segunda last season.
  • Small samples lie. A player with 200 minutes and one foul reads as a high rate. The 450-minute filter is on by default for that reason; turn it off knowing what you are seeing.
  • Fair odds are a model, not a forecast. They exclude the margin every real price carries. Beating them is not the same as beating the bookmaker.

Where the data comes from

  • Players — API-Football, fetched one club at a time so a squad is a squad rather than a page of a league.
  • Club card rates, and every referee RATE — football-data.co.uk match records, public domain, free, no key. Every Spanish match with both sides' cards and fouls.
  • Referee NAMES — API-Football, and this is the one thing on this desk that is bought. The free records above carry every card and every foul in Spain but have never named an official, in any of 33 seasons: it is a quirk of that source covering British football, not a fact about Spain. So the name is taken from one fixtures call for the completed season and joined onto the free rows by date and both clubs, after which every published rate is computed from the free data exactly as it is for the English desks. The paid dependency is a single column, not the foundation.
