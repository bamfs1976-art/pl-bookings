## Tests and CI

`python3 scripts/build_data.py` is the **front door to the pipeline**: one command, one exit code, over the same scripts `data-refresh.yml` calls in the order that workflow established — including the one that is load-bearing (`build_club_splits.py` must follow `build_pl_data.py`, or the venue splits are regenerated away and `caH`/`caA` ship null). `--dry-run` prints the plan, `--with-keyed` includes the API-Football legs, and a step whose credential is absent is **skipped and named** rather than failed, because "the refresh completed" while quietly doing a third of the work is how a dataset goes stale unnoticed. `scripts/check-build-data.mjs` fails CI if it and the workflow ever disagree.

`node scripts/gen-favicons.mjs` draws the 16 and 32px icons with **no dependencies** — Node ships zlib and a PNG is a header plus a deflated bitmap. They are drawn *for* their size rather than scaled from the 512: at 16px the lettered artwork is illegible and the fanned cards collapse into a smudge, so the small mark opens the proportions out to one yellow card with the red fanned behind it. The 180/192/512 lettered artwork still comes from `scripts/gen-icons.mjs` (needs `sharp`).

`node scripts/check-match-record.mjs` guards the **match-level record** (`plb_match_predictions`): that it is priced with the pages' own constants — shrinkage, the card lines, the booking-points lines, the derby boost, all read out of `eflc.html`/`laliga.html` rather than restated — that each desk's `DERBIES` block still parses, that over-line probabilities stay monotonic, and that the outcome convention (10 a yellow, 25 a red, a second yellow scored as the dismissal it is) holds against hand-worked scorelines. It runs the real functions on the real fixture list; a page that changes a line or a shrinkage fails it by name. `node scripts/check-appointments.mjs` guards the published-appointments overlay: that it is still present in the committed fixture list, and that what is written there still resolves to a referee with a card record. Both failure modes are silent on the page — a fixture reads "appointed" and prices at a neutral referee — so it runs in CI **and** in the fixtures workflow, which is the job that rewrites the file. `node tests/test-core.mjs` unit-tests the pure logic in `assets/core.js` (risk formula, name normalisation, pick P/L + ROI, implied-probability mapping). `node tests/test-libs.mjs` covers the four library-backed modules: that the referee leg prices a thin official at the 3.71 pivot rather than at his own three-game record, that a promoted club with no fouls-drawn record gets a neutral factor rather than the league average wearing its name, that the backtest cannot see a match's own result, that the CSV import reads a blank fouls cell as `null` and never as `0`, and that the vendored library blocks are whole and correctly attributed. Every one of those failures is silent. `python3 data/test_leagues.py` covers the league registry and the league-aware referee build — that the Premier League still reads the mirror first, that the origin's blank rows and latin-1 names are handled, that a per-league match floor applies, and that a league whose records carry no referee is refused rather than shipped as an empty ranking. GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs the tests, `node --check` over the Netlify functions / service worker / inline scripts, and the data guard on every push.
